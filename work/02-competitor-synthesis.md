## 2. Common patterns (3+ pages), market expectations we should match
- Pay on delivery and free delivery (all four).
- A two-part or combo story (inside plus outside) is the norm in 3 of 4.
- Pain-and-embarrassment opening, then "tried everything" failure story, then "without surgery".
- Text testimonials with name and state.
- A multi-tier package ladder where the bigger pack is framed as "full treatment".
- "Natural / no side effects" claim, and NAFDAC mention (3 of 4, with no number shown).
- The form is at the bottom of a long page.

## 3. Unique ideas worth borrowing (and improving)
- **P3 day-by-day timeline** (Day 1/3/7/14–30). It gives a believable expectation curve. We can borrow it with honest, hedged timings.
- **P3 "serious buyers only" notice** reduces POD no-shows. Keep, but word it politely.
- **P3 grade 1–4 self-diagnosis** is a strong engagement device. We can keep the staging but drop the cancer claim.
- **P3 how-to-use section** (capsule dose, steam/direct balm) answers a real objection.
- **P4 ingredients named** is rare and builds trust.
- **P4 discreet packaging and confidentiality line** speaks to the shame barrier.
- **P5 WhatsApp-screenshot testimonials** are the most believable format.
- **P5 short length** (20 screens) is closest to "not too long".
- **P2 story with an escalation arc** (blood, weakness, doctor, relief).

## 4. Weaknesses and gaps (our opportunity)
- **Nobody prints a NAFDAC number**, and none show a certificate. This is the easiest trust win.
- **Fake urgency everywhere** (resetting timers, auto-dated deadlines). Visitors have seen it.
- **No FAQ** on any page. None answers "can I use it with BP medicine / during pregnancy / how long / does bleeding mean cancer".
- **No clear "when to see a doctor" safety note.** Nobody does this, and it would stand out as credible.
- **No ingredients and no usage details** (except P3's usage).
- **No WhatsApp click-to-chat, no sticky mobile CTA** (except P2's top bar). A big gap for a market that runs on WhatsApp.
- **Absolute claims** ("permanent", "clinically proven", "98% success", "cancer") with zero evidence.
- **Messy offers**: P5's 3-pack is cheaper than its 2-pack, P2 mislabels, P3 has 5 tiers.
- **Weak form hygiene**: free-text state fields, mandatory email, typos ("WJATSAPP"), up to 9 fields.
- **Placeholder photos / stock doctors** (P2).

## 5. Market sophistication
This is a **highly saturated, stage-3/4** market: the claims "permanent cure", "no surgery", "NAFDAC approved", "root cause" and "free balm" are all familiar, and the audience has probably been burned by one or more of them. A new "another herbal cure" page will be ignored. What looks fresh is the opposite posture:
- **Credible, specific relief rather than a cure**: "calm the pain and bleeding so you can sit and sleep again".
- **Proof over claims**: NAFDAC number, ingredient list, real WhatsApp screenshots.
- **Honesty cues**: a "see a doctor if…" note, honest timelines, a "this is who it's not for" line.
- **The 'why previous things failed' mechanism** (P3's inside/outside idea) in a version that doesn't overclaim.
- **Shame relief and privacy** (discreet packaging, no awkward call).
These must be confirmed against our product's actual evidence. We cannot yet say our product does any of this.

## 6. Typical offer structure (Nigeria, pile, 2022 to 2026)
- Entry packs ₦15,000 to ₦25,000, mid ₦30,000 to ₦45,000, full course ₦48,000 to ₦63,500.
- Bundles add a "free" second product (balm / detox / capsules), so the "free" item is really priced in.
- Crossed-out "normal price" at 40 to 60% off (P3).
- Free delivery, pay on delivery, delivery 1 to 4 days, a guarantee of 3 to 30 days (or none).
- **Format-specific vs format-agnostic:** structure, story, proof and offer patterns hold for any format. Dose/usage instructions, the inside/outside mechanism and "kit" bundling depend on our format (**unknown, we have not been told it**).

## 7. Addendum: what this means for a TEA (our format)
- **Format-agnostic patterns we can use:** POD + free delivery, a package ladder, text/WhatsApp testimonials, a day-by-day expectation timeline, a "how to use" block, a short form at the bottom, a guarantee.
- **Format-specific patterns we cannot copy:** the inside-capsule/outside-balm mechanism (P2, P3, P5) and balm usage (steam, direct). Our product is **one tea, taken by mouth.** The tea format is actually an advantage for trust: an ingredient list of two named herbs, a one-line routine ("one teabag, 200 ml, 10 minutes"), and a gentle, non-medical feel. The weakness is the doubt: **"Tea, ke?"**.
- **Closest competitor on honesty:** P4 (ingredients listed) and P5 (short page, WhatsApp screenshots). Nobody else names their herbs.
- **Our research aligns with the gap:** the avatar document asks for exactly what all 4 pages skip: a real label, an honest tone, a private chat and "when to see a doctor".
- **Caution:** every competitor makes "cure/permanent" claims. Our research bans them. Fine. That is the differentiator, but we have **no proof assets yet**, so the page must work on honesty, transparency and a low-risk offer rather than on testimonials.
