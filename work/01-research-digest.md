## 2. The core truth
"**Piles are not the real problem. Silence is.**" Four layers: **body, mind, social (shame), money.** Competitors only speak to the body.

## 3. Pains and symptoms, in their words
- Toilet: "I literally pray not to use the toilet everyday which is not possible." / "I would struggle in the toilet and cry a lot after using the toilet." / "I envy people that would just enter toilet, defecate and come out freely."
- Seat: shifting, sitting on one side, cushions, avoiding long meetings and trips, "walks like an old man" by evening.
- Body: burning, itching (worse at night), swelling, a lump that comes and goes, blood on tissue, aching after sitting, broken sleep.
- Pidgin: "E dey pain me." "I no fit sit." "E dey burn me." "Abeg help me." "I dey fear toilet." (drafts, native-speaker check needed)
- Search phrases: "my piles are painful", "my piles keep coming back", "my piles won't go away", "piles treatment without surgery", "piles itching at night".

## 4. Fears and embarrassments
- **Shame/silence:** "been suffering n smiling coz nobody seems to understand", "I am dying in silence", "Atleast i know am not alone".
- **Surgery:** "i know 2 people that allegedly died from medical surgery", "Been thinking of doing the surgery but it scares me to the core", recovery "was hell" the first 3 days; cost ("I do not have enough money to undergo the surgery yet"); downtime from work.
- **Seriousness:** "Is it cancer?" "Can piles kill you?" "Is the blood from piles or something else?" (a doctor warned of a 7-month "pile" that was something else.)
- **Fertility and sex myths:** "I won't be able to impregnate a lady."
- **Being cheated again** by herbal sellers: "countless drugs and fake agbos that added no value but more trauma".
- **Family:** a daughter/son who saw their dad cry for the first time.

## 5. What they have tried and why it failed
Chemist pain tablets, creams, warm-water sitting, more water, fibre, hospital drugs (Daflon about ₦1,500 for 15 tablets), a Jumia product (₦2,500), a ₦30,000 scan, agbo and alagbo, bitter-leaf juice, prayers. It failed because the relief was temporary, the sellers all sound the same ("Permanent cure with less than N500", "Tested and trusted"), and "the money wasted can't just be mentioned".

## 6. Desired outcome
Not "cure". It is the simple wish: **"just enter, do your business and come out freely"**, sit without a plan, sleep through the night, stop hiding it, stop paying for another fake. Gratitude language: "I can sit again", "I poo without pain and with ease."

## 7. Objections (and what they hide)
"I have tried everything." "Is it real or another scam?" "**What is inside?**" "**Tea, ke?**" "**Is it registered?**" "Can I use it with my drugs?" "Pregnant/breastfeeding?" "How long before I feel something?" "Is it expensive?" "I'm afraid to tell anyone." "I'll just go to hospital" (fair, never argue).

## 8. Language and where conversations happen
Plain English plus light Pidgin; medical words only for the educated; religious comfort ("God will do it", respect it); gallows humour ("Attach picture", **do not copy**). Channels: YouTube comments, Nairaland, Facebook mothers' groups, WhatsApp status/groups, Google, Instagram/TikTok. The call to action everywhere is **"DM us"**: a private, human chat.

## 9. What the research tells the brand to do and avoid
- **Do:** start with the feeling, say "piles", show what is inside the cup (Dongoyaro, Negro pepper), say how to use it, invite a private chat, reply to every message.
- **Never:** "miracle", "permanent cure", "100%", joke at sufferers, fake customer stories, copy seller scripts, discourage hospital, graphic or shaming images, or say "skip surgery".
- **Always route to DM:** pregnancy, breastfeeding, children, heavy bleeding, other medicines, surgery decisions.
- Approved benefit wording in the bank: "made to help ease pain when passing stool, soothe itching and burning, help reduce swelling and irritation, keep you comfortable sitting, walking and working", and "made to help soothe irritated tissue, improve blood flow and reduce inflammation".
