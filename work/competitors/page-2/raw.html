Causes of Pile (Hemorrhoids)

Pile, also known as hemorrhoids, occurs when the veins in the anus and lower rectum become swollen and inflamed. Several factors can contribute to this condition, including:

  • Chronic Constipation or Straining – Pushing too hard during bowel movements increases pressure on the rectal veins, causing them to swell.
  • Prolonged Sitting – Sitting for long periods, especially on hard surfaces, puts pressure on the anal area and can lead to hemorrhoids.
  • Low-Fiber Diet – A diet lacking fiber leads to hard stools, making bowel movements painful and increasing the risk of pile.
  • Pregnancy & Childbirth – The increased pressure from the growing baby and the strain of delivery can cause hemorrhoids in pregnant women.
  • Obesity – Excess weight, especially around the abdomen, puts pressure on the rectal veins.
  • Heavy Lifting – Frequent lifting of heavy objects can strain the lower body and contribute to hemorrhoids.
  • Aging – As people grow older, the tissues supporting the veins in the rectum weaken, making them more prone to swelling.
  • Genetics (Hereditary Factors) – If your parents or close family members have had hemorrhoids, you may be more likely to develop them.
  • Dehydration – Not drinking enough water results in hard stools, making bowel movements painful and straining the anal veins.
  • Frequent Use of Laxatives or Suppositories – Overuse of laxatives can weaken the bowel muscles, making natural bowel movements difficult and increasing the risk of hemorrhoids.
