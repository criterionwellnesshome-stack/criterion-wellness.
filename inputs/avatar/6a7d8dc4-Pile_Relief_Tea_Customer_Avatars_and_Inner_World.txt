How to read this. The eight avatars are composites. Names are placeholders. Every fear,
habit and phrase comes from real public posts, comments and Nigerian news: YouTube
comments on a 101K-view piles vlog, Nairaland threads, a Facebook mothers' page, Google

quotation marks are real quotes. Words in italics without quotation marks are the inner
thoughts we think these people have, written from what they say (our reading, not their
words).


1. The big picture

The one truth under everything
Piles are not the real problem. Silence is. A person can handle pain. What breaks them is
carrying it alone, smiling in public, and being afraid to say the word.

        "been suffering n smiling coz nobody seems to understand"
        "I am dying in silence" (opening line of a Nairaland thread by a man who had piles since
        secondary school)
        "Nobody knows the pain people go thru on a daily."


Four layers of the problem

   Layer          What it looks like

   Body           Pain at the toilet, burning, itching, a lump, blood on
                  the tissue, aching after sitting

   Mind           Fear of the toilet, fear it is something worse, fear it
                  will never go

   Social         Shame, jokes, myths ("you won't be able to
                  impregnate a woman"), hiding it from spouse and
                  friends

   Money          Scans, drugs, agbo, surgery. "The money wasted
                  can't just be mentioned."

Our content must touch all four. Most competitors only talk about the body.


Why people end up with sellers
   1. Public hospitals feel slow or unhelpful. "It's so unfortunate that all these our general
        hospitals don't give actual treatment to every ailments."
   2. Surgery is scary. "i know 2 people that allegedly died from medical surgery."
   3. Surgery is costly. "I do not have enough money to undergo the surgery yet."
   4. Everyone has a herb to recommend. "Nigerians always suggest the native solution over
        medicals."
   5. They hope a cheap natural thing will finally work.


Why they stay disappointed
        Seller language is loud and the same everywhere: "Before you have that surgery try
        this herb it works." "Permanent cure with less than N500." "Tested and trusted."
        Replies from the crowd: "countless drugs and fake agbos that added no value but more
        trauma."
        A Nigerian surgeon in Premium Times: herbal cure claims are hard to verify.

The hole in the market: a calm, specific, real brand that tells them what is in the cup and
speaks to the shame.

3. The pain map (all the things piles do to a life)
At the toilet: fear before going; holding it in; pain while passing; pain after; crying
afterwards; blood on tissue; blood in the bowl; fear of the next time.

On the seat: shifting; avoiding chairs; sitting on one side; cushions; avoiding long
meetings; avoiding long trips; walking stiff.

Around the clock: burning; itching, especially at night; swelling; a lump that comes and
goes; aching after sitting; broken sleep.

In relationships: hiding from spouse; avoiding sex; fear about fertility; avoiding friends;
jokes that hurt.

In money and work: lost hours; delayed treatments; spending on drugs, agbo, scans; fear
of surgery cost.

In the mind: shame, anxiety, tiredness, loneliness, fear of cancer, fear of never being
normal.


4. The worry map
        Is it cancer? (A Nigerian doctor on X told of a man whose "piles" went for seven
        months before a scan showed something else.)
        Will it ever go away?
        Will it come back after surgery?
        Will surgery kill me?
        Can I still have children?
        Is the blood from piles or something else?
        Am I losing too much blood? (A Nigerian surgeon warns of anaemia, dizziness and
        fainting from long blood loss.)
        Who can I tell?
        Which herb is real?

6. The challenges they face every day
   1. Finding reliable information that is Nigerian, not foreign.
   2. Telling real herbs from fake ones.
   3. Affording care. A scan of about N30,000 was mentioned by one poster; a recovered
        poster priced Daflon at N1,500 for 15 tablets and a Jumia product at N2,500.
   4. Taking time off work.
   5. Finding private care. The shame stops them from visiting.
   6. Managing the bowel: constipation and straining keep triggering the problem.
   7. Long-sitting jobs and routines they cannot change.
   8. Sorting myths: "it stops you from having a child", "it is a spiritual thing", "surgery kills".

8. How they talk
        Pidgin and simple English. "E dey pain me." "I no fit sit." "Abeg help me."
        Medical words used by the educated. Hemorrhoids, haemorrhoids, prolapse,
        banding, laser.
        Religious comfort. "God will do it." "Keep me in prayers." Respect it.
        Gallows humour. "Attach picture." Do not copy it. Use warmth.
        Shame words. "Dying in silence." "Nobody knows." "Embarrassing."


9. What we must never do
        Joke at the sufferer.
        Use "miracle", "permanent cure" or "100%".
        Pretend a story is a real customer.
        Copy the seller scripts: "Before you have that surgery try this herb."
        Discourage hospital. We are a gentle partner, not a replacement.
        Show graphic or shaming images.
