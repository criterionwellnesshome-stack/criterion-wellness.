Story 1: The Man at the Back of the Bus
Tunde drives a bus. Twelve hours a day. He knows every pothole by name. He knows the
exact bump that makes him close his eyes. He has told no one. Not his wife, not his
friends. At the garage he laughs with the others. At home he walks slowly to the toilet,
listens at the door, and breathes before he enters.

One night his wife says, "You walk like an old man." He laughs. He says it is the road. She
says nothing.

The next day a packet appears on the kitchen table. A box of tea. A note: "One a day. Just
try."

He does not say thank you. He drinks it the next morning.

This is a scene, not a real customer. If this is someone you love, you can start the same way.


Story 2: The Woman in the Corner Office
Amaka has a good job. She smiles in meetings and has the best lines in the team chat. At 3
p.m. she shifts in her chair. At 5 p.m. she tells herself she will see a doctor soon. At 9 p.m.
she types into Google: "my piles are painful".

She closes the tab. Opens it again. Closes it.

She wants someone to say it is normal to feel this way. She wants someone to say it is not
her fault.

Here is the sentence: It is not your fault, and you do not have to hide.

A scene, not a real customer.

Long post 1: "What no one tells you about piles"
Piles are common and nobody wants to talk about them. Here is what we have learned
from reading hundreds of public comments, from Nigerian forums to YouTube videos.

First: people suffer in silence. One person wrote, "been suffering n smiling coz nobody
seems to understand." Another wrote, "I am dying in silence."

Second: fear is half the problem. Fear of the toilet. Fear of blood. Fear of surgery. Fear
that it is something worse. Fear of being laughed at.

Third: money matters. Drugs, creams, scans, agbo, lost days. One poster said the money
wasted could not even be mentioned.

Long post 3: "The hidden cost of piles" (good for LinkedIn)
Piles are a health problem, a productivity problem and a quiet money problem.

A person who cannot sit comfortably works less, concentrates less and avoids meetings.
People who drive or ride for a living lose income on bad days. Drugs, creams, scans and
herbs add up. Some people lose confidence and avoid relationships.

In our research we saw one person say that a hospital asked for a scan of about N30,000
before anything else. Another listed a routine of drugs, a suppository and a Jumia product
costing a few thousand naira. A third said the money spent on herbs and alagbo "can't just
be mentioned."

We think the conversation needs to start earlier and be warmer. That is why we are
building honest content and a simple product. If you manage a team, a fleet or a shop, the
easiest first step is to make it normal to talk.
